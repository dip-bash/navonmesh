McKinsey & Company has become one of the first major professional services firms to formally embed AI collaboration into its hiring process. Graduate recruits now encounter the firm's internal AI tool, "Lilli," during final-round assessments — and how well they work alongside the system is treated as a meaningful evaluation signal, not a side note.

CEO Bob Sternfels disclosed that McKinsey currently operates a virtual workforce of approximately 20,000 AI agents running in parallel with its 40,000 human employees. These agents handle tasks spanning research synthesis, slide generation, data modeling, and client deliverable drafting — areas that were almost entirely human-led just two years ago.

The development represents a broader inflection point in how elite professional firms think about talent. The ability to delegate effectively to AI, verify its outputs, and integrate them into client-ready work is emerging as a core professional skill — one that cannot be learned on the job in the traditional sense but must be demonstrated at the point of hiring.

For business school graduates and young professionals, the message is pointed: firms are no longer hiring for what you can do alone, but for what you can accomplish in collaboration with intelligent systems.
