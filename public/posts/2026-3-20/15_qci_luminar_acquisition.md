Quantum Computing Inc. has officially closed its $110 million all-cash acquisition of Luminar Semiconductor, transforming the company into a vertically integrated photonics business that controls the full signal chain from chip design through system deployment. The deal had been announced earlier this year and received regulatory clearance this week.

The acquisition gives QCi ownership of Luminar's silicon photonics fabrication capabilities — a critical asset as photonic approaches to quantum computing gain commercial traction due to their ability to operate at room temperature, unlike superconducting qubit systems that require extreme cooling. Vertical integration allows QCi to optimize across hardware layers in ways that fabless competitors cannot.

In parallel, QCi finalized a separate $5 million acquisition of NuCrypt, a firm specializing in quantum key distribution and quantum communications hardware. The combination positions QCi to address both quantum computing and quantum networking markets — two segments that are increasingly viewed as complementary as enterprises begin planning for post-quantum cryptography transitions.

QCi reported a cash position of $1.52 billion following the acquisitions, providing meaningful runway to execute its integration strategy and invest in next-generation photonic chip development. Analysts noted that the company's aggressive M&A posture reflects a broader consolidation trend in the quantum sector, where the capital and engineering complexity required to compete is beginning to favor well-funded vertically integrated players over narrow specialists.
