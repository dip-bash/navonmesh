Micron Technology reported second-quarter revenue of $23.86 billion, beating analyst expectations and marking a sharp year-over-year increase driven almost entirely by surging demand for high-bandwidth memory from AI data center operators. The company also announced it will increase 2026 capital expenditure to more than $25 billion — a $5 billion upward revision — as it races to expand production of HBM3E, the memory architecture at the heart of AI training and inference chips.

As one of only three global suppliers of high-bandwidth memory alongside SK Hynix and Samsung, Micron occupies a strategically critical position in the AI supply chain. Every Nvidia Blackwell GPU shipped requires a substantial allocation of HBM, and demand is currently outpacing supply across the industry.

Despite the strong results, Micron's stock declined after the announcement — a reaction analysts attributed to investor concern about the pace and scale of capital commitments at a time when AI hardware spending cycles are notoriously difficult to predict. There is also ongoing uncertainty about whether DRAM pricing will remain elevated or compress as competing supply comes online through 2026 and 2027.

The earnings report nonetheless reinforces the central role memory plays in the AI hardware stack, and positions Micron as one of the clearest financial beneficiaries of the infrastructure buildout — even as the competitive and capital dynamics remain complex.
