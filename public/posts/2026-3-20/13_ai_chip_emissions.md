A Bloomberg analysis has found that the accelerating production of AI memory chips is on track to push semiconductor manufacturing emissions to approximately 247 million metric tons of CO₂ equivalent by 2030 — a 33% increase from current levels. The projection adds a largely overlooked dimension to the climate cost of AI: while public attention has focused on the energy consumption of data centers, the fabrication of the chips that power those data centers is itself a major and growing source of industrial emissions.

Chip fabs — particularly those producing advanced DRAM and NAND flash — rely heavily on energy-intensive processes including photolithography, chemical vapor deposition, and ultra-pure water systems. Many of the largest fabs, especially in Taiwan, South Korea, and Japan, still draw significant portions of their power from fossil fuel grids, limiting the impact of renewable energy procurement pledges made at the corporate level.

The findings create a new pressure point for semiconductor companies already navigating geopolitical supply chain risks and capital intensity. Investors and regulators increasingly focused on Scope 3 emissions — those embedded in supply chains — are likely to push AI companies to account for fabrication emissions as part of their overall climate footprint.

Some analysts see the disclosure as a catalyst for accelerating investment in green fab technology, including hydrogen-based processes and next-generation lithography that reduces chemical usage. However, the commercial incentives to prioritize yield and throughput over sustainability remain dominant in the near term.
