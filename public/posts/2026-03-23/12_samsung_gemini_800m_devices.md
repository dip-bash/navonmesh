Samsung Electronics has announced an ambitious target to double the number of its mobile devices running Google's Gemini AI, aiming to reach 800 million units by the close of 2026. The goal represents a significant acceleration of the partnership between Samsung and Google, which has seen Gemini integrated into Galaxy flagship devices as the default AI assistant in place of Bixby for a growing range of functions.

The 800 million figure would make Samsung's device fleet one of the largest single deployment channels for any AI model on the planet, dwarfing the subscriber bases of most standalone AI applications. The integration spans Galaxy smartphones, tablets, and increasingly wearables — embedding Gemini capabilities directly into hardware at the point of manufacture rather than relying on app-layer installation.

For Google, the Samsung partnership is a critical hedge against the scenario where Apple's revamped Siri — also now powered by Gemini under a separate agreement — draws users away from standalone Google AI products. Having Gemini as the default intelligence layer on both the world's largest Android OEM and eventually Apple's iOS ecosystem would give Google AI unprecedented reach across the global smartphone installed base.

For Samsung, the deeper Gemini integration represents a strategic acknowledgment that the AI assistant arms race is now a core hardware differentiator. Flagship device buyers increasingly factor AI capabilities — on-device processing speed, assistant intelligence, real-time translation, and camera AI — into purchase decisions alongside traditional metrics like screen quality and battery life.
