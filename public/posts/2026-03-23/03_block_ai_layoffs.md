Block, the company behind Square, Cash App, and Afterpay, announced in late February that it would cut more than 4,000 employees — reducing its global workforce from over 10,000 to just under 6,000. CEO Jack Dorsey explicitly cited AI as the driver, not financial pressure. Block had just reported Q4 gross profit of $2.87 billion, up 26% year-over-year, with Cash App growing 33%. The cuts are proactive, not reactive.

In a shareholder letter and a post on X, Dorsey made his position clear: "Intelligence tools have changed what it means to build and run a company. A significantly smaller team, using the tools we're building, can do more and do it better. And intelligence tool capabilities are compounding faster every single week." He predicted that within a year, the majority of companies would reach the same conclusion. Block's stock rose more than 24% following the announcement.

The ripple effects are spreading fast. Within weeks, reports emerged that Meta was weighing a 20% global headcount reduction — framing the decision in similar language about AI-enabled efficiency and elite team concentration. Amazon, Microsoft, and Verizon had already made sweeping cuts in the preceding year with AI cited as a structural factor, not a one-time correction. Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman warned that white-collar workers have 12 to 18 months before facing widespread displacement pressure.

Analysts and economists are divided on whether Dorsey's move represents a genuine inflection in AI-driven restructuring or a right-sizing dressed in AI narrative. Oxford Economics data showed that many recent AI-attributed layoffs tracked more closely with pandemic-era overhiring corrections. But Anton Korinek, an economist studying AI's labor impact, told Fortune: "Once a few companies start the trend, competitive forces may induce others to follow suit."
