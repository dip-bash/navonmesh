Meta is said to be actively evaluating a workforce reduction of up to 20% globally, according to multiple reports this week — a move that analysts say would save $2 to $4 billion in 2026 and $5 to $8 billion in 2027. The cuts, if confirmed, would come on top of Meta's already aggressive AI infrastructure spending: Zuckerberg has guided for $115 to $135 billion in capital expenditure in 2026, much of it directed at data centers and AI compute.

The logic mirrors Jack Dorsey's reasoning at Block, but with a different organizational philosophy. Rather than a clean bulk reduction, Meta is said to be concentrating headcount on elite AI researchers and engineering talent while flattening roles that can be augmented or replaced by AI-assisted workflows. Zuckerberg has been explicit in recent earnings calls that "projects that used to require big teams can now be accomplished by a single very talented person" — a formulation that positions AI as a multiplier for star performers and a replacement for supporting layers.

Meta recently acquired AI startup Manus for at least $2 billion, adding another capability layer to its in-house AI tooling. The company is also investing heavily in its AI assistant across WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook — positioning the product as a potential competitor to ChatGPT and Google Gemini in consumer markets, backed by Meta's uniquely vast social graph and direct access to billions of users.

Wall Street analysts note that the savings from any headcount reduction are unlikely to be returned to shareholders in the near term — they are expected to be recycled directly into AI infrastructure spending. The $600 billion data center buildout Meta has outlined through 2028 remains the company's defining bet: that whoever controls the most capable AI infrastructure at scale will dominate the next generation of consumer and enterprise software.
