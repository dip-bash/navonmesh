A joint law enforcement operation involving the US Department of Justice, Canada, and Germany has seized the command-and-control infrastructure behind four significant IoT botnets: Aisuru, Kimwolf, JackSkid, and Mossad. Together, the four networks had compromised more than three million IoT devices globally and launched hundreds of thousands of distributed denial-of-service attacks — including record-breaking attacks measured at approximately 30 terabits per second, among the largest DDoS events ever recorded.

The Aisuru botnet is of particular technical note: in October 2025, it seeded Kimwolf using a novel infection technique that allowed malware to propagate through devices on internal networks — a capability that significantly accelerates the rate of device compromise compared to conventional internet-facing scanning methods. The operation's reach extended to devices in residential, enterprise, and critical infrastructure environments across multiple continents.

Private sector cooperation was extensive. Akamai, Amazon Web Services, Cloudflare, DigitalOcean, Google, Lumen, Nokia, Okta, Oracle, PayPal, SpyCloud, and several specialized threat intelligence firms all contributed to the investigation, providing visibility into botnet traffic patterns, command-and-control infrastructure mapping, and device telemetry. The scale of industry collaboration reflects how the threat of massive botnets now affects cloud providers and internet infrastructure operators directly.

Two individuals have been identified as prime suspects: a 22-year-old Canadian national and a 15-year-old in Germany. The arrests of minors and young adults in connection with large-scale botnet operations is a recurring pattern in IoT-focused cybercrime, reflecting the accessibility of botnet-as-a-service toolkits and the relatively low technical barrier to entry for operating DDoS infrastructure once initial access tools are acquired.
