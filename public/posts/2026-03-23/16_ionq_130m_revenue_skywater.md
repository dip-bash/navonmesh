IonQ has reported $130 million in full-year 2025 GAAP revenue — a 202% year-over-year increase and a historic milestone: it is the first pure-play quantum computing company ever to report more than $100 million in annual GAAP revenue. The result exceeded the midpoint of IonQ's own prior guidance by 20% and exceeded analyst consensus estimates, marking a shift from the sector's long-running narrative of promise over performance. More than 60% of revenue came from commercial customers, and international sales accounted for over 30% of the total.

IonQ guided for 2026 revenue of $225 to $245 million — roughly doubling again — driven by system sales to global research institutions, growth in cloud-based quantum-as-a-service adoption through Amazon Braket and Azure Quantum, and expanding government contracts under sovereign quantum initiatives in the US and Europe. The company ended 2025 with $3.3 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and investments, a position strengthened by its January 2026 announcement of a $1.8 billion acquisition of chipmaker SkyWater Technology — a move designed to bring semiconductor fabrication in-house and reduce IonQ's dependence on third-party fabs for its trapped-ion processors.

The SkyWater acquisition is the strategic centerpiece of IonQ's transition from a research-stage company to an industrialized quantum hardware manufacturer. By controlling its own fabrication, IonQ can accelerate design iteration cycles, protect proprietary process steps, and control yields in a way that fabless quantum companies cannot. Wall Street analysts have shifted decisively to buy ratings following the acquisition announcement, viewing it as the final piece needed to de-risk IonQ's manufacturing roadmap.

The $100 million revenue milestone matters symbolically as much as financially. It demonstrates that at least one quantum company has found repeatable commercial traction with paying enterprise and government customers — a proof point the sector has needed to graduate from speculative to fundable on traditional institutional investment criteria.
