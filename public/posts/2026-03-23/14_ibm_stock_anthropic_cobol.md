IBM's stock is sitting roughly 20% below its 52-week high following a sharp sell-off triggered in late February, when Anthropic announced advances in its AI tools' ability to handle COBOL programming — the decades-old language that still underpins much of the financial sector's core banking infrastructure. IBM derives a significant portion of its services revenue from supporting enterprises that run COBOL-based systems, and investors reacted as though Anthropic's progress represented a near-term threat to that business.

Several analysts this week are arguing that the market has overreacted. IBM's COBOL services involve far more than code maintenance — the company's value lies in understanding the business logic, compliance requirements, and workflow dependencies embedded in decades-old system architectures, none of which can be replaced by a coding AI alone. More likely, the argument goes, AI tools will make IBM's COBOL service engineers more productive rather than redundant, expanding IBM's capacity to serve clients rather than eliminating the need for IBM's involvement.

At a price-to-earnings ratio of around 23x, IBM trades below Nvidia's 36x while offering a more diversified revenue profile spanning hybrid cloud, consulting, software, and quantum computing. IBM's quantum computing division remains one of its most strategically significant long-term assets — a technology with direct relevance to AI compute requirements as quantum systems edge toward practical advantage.

For growth-at-a-reasonable-price investors watching the AI infrastructure space, the COBOL-driven dip in IBM is drawing fresh interest from those who believe the company's long history of technology transitions — from hardware to services, from on-premise to cloud — positions it to navigate the AI era without the existential disruption the market briefly priced in.
