Cybersecurity firm Dragos has released its 2026 OT/ICS Cybersecurity Report, identifying three newly tracked adversary groups that have advanced from initial reconnaissance to active operational disruption across industrial control environments globally.

The most concerning finding involves a group tracked as KAMACITE, which has been systematically mapping control loops across US critical infrastructure — cataloguing alarm configurations, process set points, and network topologies in a pattern consistent with preparation for future sabotage rather than immediate attack. A second group, ELECTRUM, has been targeting distributed energy systems in Poland, probing the edges of the European power grid at a time of heightened geopolitical tension.

The third group, VOLTZITE, has been elevated to Stage 2 of Dragos's ICS Cyber Kill Chain — signifying that it has progressed beyond reconnaissance and is now actively extracting engineering configuration files and alarm data in order to understand the precise conditions that would trigger process shutdowns. This level of operational knowledge is a prerequisite for causing targeted physical damage rather than simple network disruption.

The report reinforces a pattern Dragos has documented over several years: adversaries are investing in long-duration, patient intelligence-gathering within OT environments rather than seeking quick wins. Industrial operators are advised to audit network segmentation, implement anomaly detection on process data historians, and review which engineering workstations have remote access enabled.
