Google has launched Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite, a new efficiency-focused model priced at just $0.25 per million tokens — making it one of the cheapest high-capability AI models available from a major lab. The release arrives with performance claims of roughly 2.5× faster response and output generation compared to its predecessors, targeting high-throughput production use cases where cost and latency matter more than raw capability.

The move is a clear signal that the competitive battleground in AI has shifted significantly. After years of racing to release the most powerful models, the leading labs are now competing just as hard on price and speed, squeezing inference economics to win the large base of enterprise customers running AI at scale across millions of requests per day.

For startups and mid-size companies building on top of AI APIs, the release creates pressure to revisit model routing strategies — using premium models only for complex reasoning tasks while offloading simpler calls to ultra-cheap models like Flash-Lite. This layered model architecture is fast becoming standard practice in production AI systems.

The broader implication: AI inference is commoditizing rapidly, and the providers who control distribution, developer tooling, and ecosystem lock-in may matter as much as those with the technically superior model.
