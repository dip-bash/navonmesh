Nvidia's four-day GTC 2026 conference wrapped up with CEO Jensen Huang making one of his boldest declarations yet: AI has crossed the threshold from experimental technology into critical infrastructure. The defining shift, Huang argued, is the move from generative AI — models that respond to prompts — toward fully autonomous agents that plan, execute, and iterate without human input at each step.

The company formally introduced the concept of "AI factories," a term for data centers purpose-built for continuous, intelligent operation rather than periodic batch compute. Huang projected that the global AI computing market could reach $1 trillion by 2027, driven by explosive growth in real-time inference demand.

On the hardware side, Nvidia showcased progress on its Blackwell and Vera Rubin chip architectures, both targeting the inference workload that now accounts for the majority of AI compute spending in production deployments. Partners including healthcare robotics firms CMR Surgical and Johnson & Johnson MedTech were highlighted as early adopters of Nvidia's new physical AI platform — bringing autonomous intelligence into operating rooms through synthetic data generation and digital twin environments.

The overarching message: the AI infrastructure race has shifted from who can train the biggest model to who can serve intelligent agents at scale, cheaply, and reliably.
