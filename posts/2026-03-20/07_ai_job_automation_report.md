A new McKinsey Global Institute report released this week finds that AI has automated approximately 12% of all job tasks across the global economy over the past two years — a faster rate of displacement than most prior estimates anticipated. The sectors most affected include legal research, medical imaging interpretation, financial analysis, and content moderation, where AI systems have taken over tasks that previously required trained professionals.

However, the same period has seen roughly 8% of new job categories emerge that are directly linked to AI — roles spanning model evaluation, AI workflow design, agent supervision, and synthetic data labeling. Net employment across the economy has remained broadly flat, though with significant redistribution across industries and skill levels.

The report cautions that aggregate stability masks meaningful inequality in impact. Workers with lower educational attainment and those in roles with highly routine cognitive tasks face disproportionate displacement risk, while workers with skills in AI collaboration, systems thinking, and domain expertise applied alongside AI tools are seeing wage growth and increased demand.

McKinsey recommends that governments prioritize retraining pipelines specifically designed around AI-adjacent skill development, and that companies invest in transition support rather than treating headcount reduction as a windfall from automation adoption.
