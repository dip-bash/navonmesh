Nvidia has secured a non-exclusive licensing agreement for Groq's AI inference chip technology and brought on several of the company's top executives — including founder Jonathan Ross — in a deal that significantly expands Nvidia's inference infrastructure capabilities. Groq will continue to operate as an independent company under new leadership, but the arrangement gives Nvidia meaningful access to Groq's architectural approaches to ultra-fast token generation.

Groq had carved out a reputation for delivering some of the fastest inference speeds available, often dramatically outpacing GPU-based systems on certain workloads through its Language Processing Unit architecture. The acquisition of talent and technology signals that Nvidia is not content to compete on raw GPU throughput alone and wants to incorporate alternative inference paradigms into its expanding portfolio.

The deal reflects the intensifying hardware competition that has emerged around inference specifically — the phase of AI deployment that now accounts for the majority of enterprise compute spending. As inference volumes grow with the rise of autonomous agents running continuously, the economics and architecture of serving AI responses at scale have become a strategic priority for every major chip company.

For the broader market, the move consolidates more inference expertise within Nvidia at a time when competitors including AMD, Intel, and a wave of AI chip startups are all targeting the same growing workload.
