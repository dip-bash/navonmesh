French quantum computing company Pasqal has announced it will go public via a merger with Nasdaq-listed blank-check company Bleichroeder Acquisition Corp. II, joining the wave of quantum firms entering US public markets in early 2026. The announcement comes weeks after IQM revealed its own SPAC deal and Infleqtion completed its NYSE debut in February, signaling that quantum computing has entered a new phase of capital market activity following years as a venture-funded deep tech category.

Pasqal builds quantum computers based on neutral-atom architecture — a modality that uses laser-cooled atoms trapped in optical lattices as qubits. Neutral-atom systems have attracted significant research interest because they allow qubits to be arranged in programmable 2D and 3D geometries and can potentially scale to very large qubit counts more readily than superconducting systems, which face significant connectivity and cooling infrastructure challenges at high qubit numbers.

The company has been one of the European quantum sector's better-capitalized players, having raised significant venture funding from investors including Quantonation, the European Investment Council, and strategic backers in the French and Gulf technology ecosystems. Pasqal operates quantum systems for pharmaceutical companies, financial institutions, and national laboratories, and has been part of France's Plan Quantique, the national quantum strategy that has committed €1.8 billion to French quantum development through 2025.

The Bleichroeder SPAC deal gives Pasqal US listing status and access to institutional capital markets at a time when quantum sector valuations are rising on the back of strong results from IonQ and sustained government investment in quantum infrastructure globally. The listing is expected to close in mid-2026, pending shareholder and regulatory approvals.
