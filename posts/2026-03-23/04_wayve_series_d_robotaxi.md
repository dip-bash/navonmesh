UK-based autonomous driving startup Wayve has secured $1.2 billion in Series D funding at an $8.6 billion valuation, in one of the largest rounds ever raised by a European AI company. Uber has taken a strategic position in the round with an option to invest an additional $300 million, deepening the two companies' relationship ahead of planned public robotaxi trials in London later in 2026. Nissan is also participating as a strategic investor, extending its existing partnership with Wayve into capital alignment.

Wayve's approach to autonomous driving differs from the sensor-heavy, high-definition mapping strategies used by Waymo and Cruise. The company builds what it calls "embodied AI" — systems that learn to drive using primarily camera input and foundation model-style generalist AI, rather than exhaustive pre-programmed rules or elaborate LiDAR-based world models. The premise is that this approach is more scalable across diverse road environments and geographies, without requiring each new market to be mapped in advance at enormous cost.

The London robotaxi trials will be among the first public deployments of fully autonomous ride-hailing in the United Kingdom, where regulatory frameworks for self-driving vehicles have been advancing through the Automated Vehicles Act. The collaboration with Uber positions Wayve to ride Uber's existing demand infrastructure — driver app, passenger network, dispatch optimization — while Wayve supplies the autonomous driving layer, a model similar to how Uber has partnered with Waymo in US markets.

The funding comes as the autonomous vehicle sector experiences a second wave of serious commercial investment following the contraction of 2023 and 2024, when several high-profile companies scaled back or shut down programs. The appetite among strategic investors like Uber and Nissan suggests the industry believes viable commercial autonomy is now close enough to justify infrastructure-level bets.
