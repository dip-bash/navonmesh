Semiconductor companies Lattice Semiconductor and SEALSQ Corp have announced a collaboration to integrate TPM-based post-quantum cryptography capabilities into select Lattice FPGA solutions, targeting the growing security requirements of edge computing environments as the industry prepares for the threat of cryptographically-relevant quantum computers. The partnership integrates Lattice's secure FPGA hardware with SEALSQ's QS7001 and QVault TPM secure Root-of-Trust — combining hardware-enforced security with post-quantum algorithm support in a single chip-level solution.

The collaboration was showcased at Embedded World 2026 in Nuremberg under the Year of Quantum Security 2026 initiative, a joint industry-government effort launched in Washington, DC to treat post-quantum security as an infrastructure and governance imperative rather than a future planning item. NIST's post-quantum cryptography standards, finalized in 2024, provide the algorithmic foundation for the integrated solution, and the partnership is positioned as a direct implementation pathway for organizations needing to protect edge devices from harvest-now-decrypt-later attacks.

SEALSQ is moving aggressively on its own production ambitions alongside the Lattice partnership. The company signed a non-binding memorandum of understanding with the Government of Gujarat and Kaynes SemiCon Private Limited to establish a post-quantum semiconductor center in India targeting annual production of up to 300 million post-quantum secure semiconductors. SEALSQ is also expanding its semiconductor operations in France with a planned $200 million investment by the end of 2026, positioning itself for demand it expects to surge as PQC adoption becomes mandatory for regulated sectors.

The urgency behind the partnership reflects a well-documented threat: adversarial state actors are believed to be harvesting encrypted data today with the intent to decrypt it once quantum computers capable of breaking RSA and elliptic curve cryptography become available. The timeline for cryptographically-relevant quantum computers remains debated, but most security agencies advise organizations to begin PQC migration now given the long lifecycle of embedded and IoT hardware.
