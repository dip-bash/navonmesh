Nvidia has published its annual State of AI report, drawing on more than 3,200 responses from companies across financial services, retail, healthcare, telecommunications, and manufacturing. The headline finding: the transition from generative AI experimentation to agentic AI deployment is now complete for a significant portion of enterprises surveyed, with AI agents running in production across functions from code development and legal document review to supply chain management and patient monitoring in clinical settings.

Forty-four percent of companies surveyed either deployed or were actively assessing AI agents in 2025 — a figure that has since converted to full-scale production rollouts in early 2026. Telecommunications had the highest agentic AI adoption rate at 48%, followed by retail and consumer packaged goods at 47%. Across all industries, the top three AI goals were creating operational efficiencies (34%), improving employee productivity (33%), and opening new revenue streams (23%).

More than half of respondents reported that improved employee productivity was among the biggest AI impacts they had observed in practice — a shift from the earlier narrative of AI as a cost-cutting tool toward AI as a performance multiplier for existing workforces. The report highlights examples including Nasdaq using AI to unify data across business units and accelerate financial market analysis, and a clinical AI tool called Mona monitoring ICU patients in real time, consolidating and visualizing patient data for nurses and doctors.

The report's framing positions 2026 as the year enterprise AI stops being a subject of strategic planning documents and becomes a measurable line item in operational performance. Companies that moved quickly on deployment in 2025 are now showing ROI; those still in evaluation risk a widening competitive gap as agentic systems compound in capability month over month.
