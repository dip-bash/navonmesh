Apple's long-awaited overhaul of Siri is once again arriving in installments rather than a single launch. After the company had internally targeted iOS 26.4 — expected in late March — as the release vehicle for its AI-rebuilt assistant, Bloomberg now reports that Apple's engineering teams have been asked to distribute the features across iOS 26.5 in May and potentially iOS 27 in September, after testing revealed the software was not ready for a full rollout.

Apple confirmed to CNBC that the revamped Siri remains on track for a 2026 release overall, without committing to a specific version. iOS 26.4 will ship with minor Siri improvements and a new AI playlist generator for Apple Music, but the transformative upgrades — on-screen awareness, deep cross-app understanding, and context-aware personal assistance — are now expected later in the year.

The delay comes despite Apple having sealed a significant technical partnership with Google, under which future Apple Foundation Models will be built on Gemini infrastructure and Google's cloud technologies. The joint statement described Apple Intelligence as continuing to run on-device and via Private Cloud Compute while leveraging Google's AI capabilities as a foundation for the new Siri. Apple CEO Tim Cook has declined to confirm a specific release window beyond "2026," keeping public expectations deliberately vague while internal teams work to stabilize the product.

The new Siri, when it does ship, will be rebuilt from scratch on large language model architecture — replacing the multi-step task-specific model pipeline that has defined Siri since its inception. Apple is not aiming for a full chatbot experience like Claude or ChatGPT, at least initially, but for an assistant that genuinely understands context, handles cross-app requests, and takes real actions on behalf of the user without requiring app launches.
