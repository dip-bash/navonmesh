Finland's IQM Quantum Computers has announced a merger with Nasdaq-listed special purpose acquisition company Real Asset Acquisition Corp., valuing IQM at $1.8 billion pre-money equity and making it the first European quantum computing company to go public. The deal is expected to close around June 2026, after which IQM's American Depositary Shares will trade on a major US exchange. The company is also evaluating a dual listing on the Helsinki Stock Exchange to retain a presence in Nordic capital markets.

The transaction will leave IQM with over $450 million in combined liquidity — drawn from RAAC's trust account, PIPE financing from institutional investors, warrant exercises, and IQM's existing $172 million cash balance. That runway is intended to fund IQM's next-generation Halocene quantum processor platform, expansion of its in-house chip fabrication facility, and acceleration of commercial deployments at high-performance computing centres globally.

IQM's commercial track record gives the listing more credibility than most quantum SPAC stories. The company has sold 21 superconducting quantum systems to 13 customers — including four of the world's top ten supercomputing centres — and delivered 15 systems, the largest publicly disclosed delivery count in the sector. It reported at least $35 million in unaudited 2025 revenue and over $100 million in order bookings visibility. Its processors achieve gate fidelities above 99.9%, and it operates a vertically integrated stack spanning chip design, fabrication, software, and cloud access.

CEO Jan Goetz framed the listing as a statement of commercial maturity: "Quantum computing is a science project no more. It is an industry where customers own, operate, and build on advanced quantum computers." IQM joins a growing cohort of quantum companies entering public markets in 2026, following Infleqtion's NYSE debut in February and Xanadu's planned Nasdaq listing before the end of March.
